OTHER INFO

A crumbling palace sunk beneath the whirlpool, where the last scion of a blood-cursed noble house rots among piles of treasure and faithful servants.

Auxor Palace.

300 years ago... The venerable Auxor family were celebrated patrons of the arcane arts, lavishing dignitaries with magical gifts plundered from across the land.

100 years ago... Desiring items of immeasurable power, an Auxor warband raided the lair of a sorcerer: the Tidecaller, high priest-king of the sahuagin of the Trench Depths. They stole his regalia and his skull, a scrying relic that carries his spirit. His dying words enacted a curse that saw the Auxor bloodline wither nearly overnight, and called their palace down into the deep after what it stole. The cliff beneath it gave way into the lake, and the whirlpool is the wound it left. Prince Matyas, their final heir, proved resilient. Instead of death, his form warped in an achingly slow process of degradation into a lumbering troll, and what remains of his mind grows cloudier by the season.

Today... Servants (nebellem) are too loyal to flee but too wise to be near the prince. Their arcanist, Drulfor, has spent decades attempting both cure and magical placation. He and the nebellem also hold the falling water of the whirlpool back from the palace with stone-magic and control water, and he is exhausted. With the palace's gold stores exhausted by his experiments, he contracts sahuagin raiders to bleed the Brightscale Basin's lakeside settlements dry, promising them fresh meat and the north of Reedhaven Lake. With no true lord, the region grows lawless and desperate. For the brave or foolish, the palace gates stand unmanned.

Getting Down.

The central whirlpool pulls everything down its throat in 3 rounds. Each round, DC 12 STR or CON or take 1d6 damage. Holding breath follows normal drowning rules. Water breathing, control water, or a rope secured to a boat grants ADV.

The Great Stair.

Below the whirlpool, the throat opens into a vast cavern. A curtain of falling water pours down one wall, held in place by the nebellem's magic. Warm mineral runoff from the Cinder Peaks lights the water a teal glow. A giant, ancient processional stair descends along the wall to the Auxor palace far below. Areas 6 and 16 open onto the cavern.

The Sunken House.

The ice in Area 6 is curse-ice, the party frozen at the moment the house fell. The nebellem and Drulfor keep the palace dry. If Drulfor dies or the nebellem are slaughtered, the water wall begins to fail and the palace floods within 1d6 hours. The pantry hole (Area 11) is the sahuagin's way up from the Trench Depths.

Place of Power.

To the sahuagin, the Trench is the Tidecaller's kingdom, and the sunken palace is his tomb-shrine full of stolen relics. Vorka's tall orange dorsal fin marks her as a claimant to the Trench throne. She believes that waking the Tidecaller's skull (Area 5) and taking the palace will make her queen of the Trench Depths and rightful ruler of the Basin. Her price from Drulfor, the north of Reedhaven Lake, is the sovereign claim the sahuagin already want.

Vorka wins. The nebellem die, the water wall collapses, and the house floods. The palace becomes a sahuagin throne and raids on Reedhaven escalate.

Drulfor holds. The house stays dry and the status quo continues, with raids funding his experiments.

Lifting the curse. Returning the skull to the Tidecaller's resting place in the Trench ends the Auxor curse and could cure Matyas, but it empowers whoever carries the skull there.

Hooks.

Reedhaven's raft-council. A cloaked lizardfolk envoy waylays the party. Sahuagin raiders have capsized rafts and burned three lakeside settlements in a month, dragging their spoils down the whirlpool. The council offers 500 gp for proof the raids will cease. The lizardfolk need allies, not rescue; they cannot contest the Trench alone.

Desperate correspondence. A letter is pinned to a Reedhaven dock board. Drulfor writes with a shaking hand for kind scholars to help him cure an affliction that has long bested him.

Rumors.

1. “Sea-raiders razed three rafts last month. All their loot went down the Drain.”

2. “The Auxors had treasure from every corner of the world. It's still down there, they say. None brave enough to claim it.”

Factions.

Nebellem. Ancestral guardian-folk of House Auxor, sworn to protect their benefactors in return for being delivered from tyranny centuries prior. They attend to Prince Matyas at any cost, clinging to the belief that a cure remains possible and that a new golden age for the family will yet come. Death at his hands is a recognised possibility, but not a sufficient deterrent. They are suspicious of intruders' intent towards the prince, but wary of conflict.

Sahuagin. A mercenary band contracted by Drulfor to raid the Basin for gold, promised fresh meat and territorial gains in return. The nebellem have failed to deliver. The sahuagin grow impatient, plotting to take the palace from its dwindling staff of retainers by force. They are led by Vorka, a conniving warlord whose bright orange dorsal fin betrays her regal blood. Typically suspicious, opportunistically hostile.

Prince Matyas Auxor. Once a handsome prince, now the sole surviving member of the Auxor family, cursed with the brutish body of a troll and a tempestuous mind. Prone to breaking through walls and ceilings in fits of rage, clobbering indiscriminately.

Drulfor. The nebellem's chief arcanist, burning through gold and sanity alike in decades of failed experiments. He teeters on losing hope, and his exhaustion makes him volatile. The overlapping magic warping the palace, the prince's pet panthers, and the golems built to placate a degrading mind are all his doing.

Lady Auxor. Prince Matyas' mother, who died decades ago from the Auxor curse. A figment of her spirit lingers in Area 6, born of her son's suffering, and intent on seeing it end.

Order of Battle.

Nebellem. Will attempt to herd intruders toward exits. If combat is heard from Area 20, nebellem (other than Drulfor) move toward Matyas' location to shield him, converging in 1d4 rounds.

Sahuagin. Aggressive, but only when tactically sound (e.g. numbers, reward). If Vorka decides to seize the palace, she will rally stragglers into a horde in 1d6 rounds.

Prince Matyas. When encountered outside Area 20, spends 2 rounds superficially searching his origin and an adjacent room for intruders before returning to his room.

Followups.

Matyas dies. Drulfor is relieved, setting out to find his own path and giving the party his decades of research (providing a hook opportunity). Word reaches Vorka fast. In 1d4 hours, she rallies an extra 2d6 sahuagin from Area 11 and moves to eliminate remaining inhabitants. Do the PCs defend the palace?

Matyas lives, unfettered. He grows stronger and more berserk with time. In 3 years, his brutality makes him the last living inhabitant.

Matyas is cured. This requires exceptional tools that LV 2 PCs likely wouldn't have access to, such as wish, or returning the Tidecaller's skull to the Trench. Matyas will be introspective after a century of half-consciousness, imploring the party to take the palace and offering himself as retainer (LV 6 Fighter). This would create an opportunity for domain play. Will the nebellem serve new lords, or only ever serve Auxors?
