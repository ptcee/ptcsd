OTHER INFO

The Kytherian halls lie buried beneath the Rimewastes, warmed by an ancient hydraulic system and fought over by bugbears, the blind Cragbairn, and treasure-hungry thugs. A 1st-3rd level Shadowdark adventure.

Background.

The Brassfire Crags once spanned the mountain range where the legendary Kytherian metalsmiths worked brass into wonders. The range now lies buried beneath the northern ice. The Kytherians mined deep into the mountains and built vast brass mechanisms beneath them, using geothermal heat and an underground hydraulic system to power their works.

As the glaciers advanced, the mountains were swallowed. The halls survived beneath the ice, sealed away for generations. A shifting ice sheet has recently opened a fissure into the ruins. Treasure hunters and villagers have begun searching the exposed caverns. Several have disappeared.

The old hydraulic system still operates. Its warmth has made the underground pools and caverns habitable, attracting creatures and desperate people from the tundra. The deeper halls remain dangerous, and the Kytherian machinery is not fully understood by anyone now living there.

Factions.

The Cragbairn. Descendants of Kytherian slaves who survived beneath the ice. Generations of darkness and isolation have left them frail, blind, and savage. They inhabit Areas 15-22 and worship the crumbling ruins with cruel blood rituals and cannibalistic feasts. They are leaderless and rudimentary but fiercely protect their halls from outsiders seeking to despoil them.

The Bugbears. A band of bugbears driven south by the worst of the Rimewastes winter. They have occupied the geothermal caverns, where the warm pools and narrow passages make a defensible lair. They wear stolen furs and scraps of hide armour, and hunt along the ravine for game, travellers, and anything foolish enough to cross their territory.

Their leader, Grakka, took command after her father was killed by the chuul. She is strong and suspicious, but not foolish. The bugbears are territorial and cruel to trespassers, though they can be bargained with when doing so serves their interests. They fear the chuul and know it moves through the flooded burrows beneath the ruins. Grakka wants the clockwork medallion as proof that she has claim to the deeper halls.

The Thugs. A haphazard group of delinquents, prospectors, and villagers chasing tales of riches and treasure. They are leaderless and entirely opportunistic, seeking to get rich without getting into danger.

The Chuul. An eight-foot-long mix of crustacean, insect, and serpent, the result of an ancient magical ritual gone awry. It mostly resides at the bottom of the ravine but has made a nest for its tadpole offspring inside Area 25. It can also move around underground between its many burrows.

Rumours.

  1. A powerful amulet is hidden deep within the halls.
  2. The Kytherians built an enormous brass mechanism capable of granting a wish.
  3. The bugbear chieftain carries a mighty enchanted spear.
  4. One of the Kytherian kings and all of his possessions were cursed.

Entrances and Environment.

Reservoir. A fissure in the tundra drops 40 feet into a 5-foot-deep freshwater pool in Area 1.

Ravine path. A narrow hunting path used by the bugbears leads to Area 8.

Skylight. A crevice in the ceiling, 50 feet above Area 6, admits faint daylight.

Ravine. A cool breeze blows through the gorge above the rushing din of a freezing river 100 feet below. A broken stone bridge spans the ravine. Directly above it is a crevice in the ice and dirt ceiling, bathing the ravine with faint light from outside.

Caverns. Humid and warm, the cave interiors smell like sulfur and rotten eggs. Ankle-deep warm water covers the slippery ground and slowly drains over the edge of the ravine. Steam occasionally seeps out of cracks in the walls. The bugbears use these caverns as shelter from the Rimewastes.

Ruins. Sounds of dripping water and the smell of rot fill the stale air. The vaulted 20-foot ceiling is carved from smooth granite, speckled with flakes of sparkling quartz that catch the light. Marble tiles in the floor and walls are decrepit and decayed.

Brass doors. Made from solid brass, 20 feet tall, 5 feet wide, and 2 feet thick. They operate on a vast underground hydraulic system. Opening a door requires a nearby brazier to be lit by placing a burning torch into its centre receptacle. The doors open one round later and stay open for as long as the torch burns in the brazier, closing one round after it is extinguished.

Burrows. A series of curving tunnels lead into the submerged underbelly of Brassfire, where a complicated hydraulic system operates. The maze-like tunnels are about 7 feet wide, pitch black, and filled with murky water. The chuul may move into and out of a burrow with ease, taking 1-4 rounds to move between burrows if in a hurry. Both sides of the sheer ravine wall are littered with hidden burrow entries.

Combat Overview.

Thugs. Sneak into Area 1 shortly after the PCs enter the dungeon. They hang around Areas 1 and 2 digging through rubble and looking for treasure. They retreat toward Area 1 if threatened and leave the dungeon if needed. They avoid combat at all costs but loot mercilessly. They mostly consist of young, simple human villagers.

Cragbairn. Keep to Areas 15-17 and 21, avoiding other areas unless attacking the bugbears. If overwhelmed, retreat to Area 21 and barricade themselves in. Only priests and other higher-ups are allowed in Area 22 during the ritual. They do not know about secret doors or what the receptacles do.

Bugbears. Mostly keep to Areas 10-13, avoiding Area 23. They check the net in Area 8 for anything caught in it and inspect or reset traps in Areas 7 and 14. They use the pools and side passages to flank intruders. If overwhelmed, they escape through Area 8 or regroup in Area 10. They fear the chuul and know about the secret door in Area 23 and the traps in Areas 7 and 14.

Chuul. An extremely cunning predator. Tries to bring live prey to a nearby burrow so it can feed it to its tadpoles in Area 25. Mostly swims in the bottom of the ravine and inside the subterranean hydraulic system. Drawn to extremely loud noises outside of the river. Retreats down into the ravine or a burrow if overwhelmed.

Running the Halls.

Grakka can be bargained with. She wants the clockwork medallion as proof of her claim to the deeper halls and leverage against the Cragbairn. The bugbears do not know the true function of the Kytherian Mechanism.

The Cragbairn and bugbears are in conflict over access to the old halls. Neither faction needs to be wholly reasonable or wholly evil.

The chuul remains the major shared threat. Both factions fear it, and its burrows let it appear in unexpected places.

The Shatter-Tooth ogres and Runt-Grown Goblins remain part of the wider Rimewastes, not replacements for either dungeon faction. The ogres know the fissure exists but have not entered.

Mammoths may be seen crossing the tundra above the ruins.

Treasure.

Room keys that call for treasure table results, roll on the random treasure tables for the party's level.

Traps and Secret Doors.

Spiked wall trap. Spring-loaded board full of sharpened sticks. DC 12 DEX or 2d6 damage.

Brass banshee trap. Tiny, flat brass disk. When stepped on, it emits a gravitic field underfoot and a long, extremely loud warning screech. DC 15 STR to escape. Dwarves are immune. DC 18 INT to re-arm; 1:6 chance, otherwise the device is destroyed.

Secret doors. Murky underwater passages and grinding, revolving walls.

Magic Items.

Frostpiercer.

A long bone-tipped spear with a dark iron head that never gathers frost. Bonus: +1 spear.

  • Benefit: Regain 1d6 HP whenever you slay a creature with this weapon. The wielder suffers no penalties for fighting in deep snow or on ice.
  • Curse: The spear must be kept near open air or cold wind every 4 hours or temporarily lose all magical properties.

Gladius of the Arena Master.

A mirror-like blade that reflects the wielder's image.

  • Bonus: +0 shortsword. Can only be wielded by a fighter.
  • Benefit: Crits on a 19 or 20.
  • Curse: Weapon constantly amplifies the wielder's bravado and pride.

Clockwork Medallion.

A tarnished brass medallion with numerous ticking clockwork mechanisms.

  • Benefit: Wearer always knows the time of day and what direction is north. Once per day, this medallion can cast the knock spell without making a spellcasting check.

Miserable Maplewand.

A gnarled old branch with a broken tip and spindly roots jutting out of the handle.

  • Benefit: This wand contains the spells invisibility and silence.
  • Curse: Each time you fail a spellcasting check with this wand, you can only whisper until completing a rest. On a critical failure, it teleports to a far-flung isolated location.
  • Personality: Miserable and senile. Only wants to be left alone with some peace and quiet.

Hammerhelm of the Thunder King.

A battle-scarred bronze helm with indigo plume and polished edges.

  • Benefit: Grants +1 AC. Attacks with maces and hammers deal an additional 1d8 damage.
  • Curse: You attract storms and bad weather.
